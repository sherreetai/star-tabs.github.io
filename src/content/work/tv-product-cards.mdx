challengeHeading: Customers needed to compare TV products while navigating complex eligibility, holdings and product dependencies.
challenge: TV add-on cards created heavy scrolling and made products difficult to compare. My role was to use research to create a clearer product-card experience and structure the CRO test so we could understand which changes improved performance.
outcome: Redesigned TV product cards contributed to a reported 21% uplift in XSUS orders, with the winning variant reaching 95% statistical confidence.

<CaseDecisionGrid compact>
  <CaseSection eyebrow="Simplifying the choice" title="Simplified the product cards" mediaSrc="/images/tv-product-cards/simplified-cards.svg" layout="centered" divider="none" paddingTop="large">
    <p>I simplified the information hierarchy to make the product, value and action easier to understand at a glance. Research showed customers preferred larger product cards and clearer CTAs, even when this meant slightly more scrolling.</p>
  </CaseSection>

  <CaseSection eyebrow="Designing from evidence" title="Used research to refine the design" mediaSrc="/images/tv-product-cards/research-testing.svg" layout="centered">
    <p>I tested different approaches with 10 Virgin Media customers without TV. 7/10 preferred Variant Y, finding the CTA clearer and valuing the reassurance that products could easily be removed from their basket.</p>
    <p>Wider research also showed customers wanted clearer product comparisons, including which channels were included rather than channel count alone.</p>
  </CaseSection>

  <CaseSection eyebrow="Handling real-world complexity" title="Designed for real customer states" layout="centered">
    <p>I co-designed the experience around different holding and eligibility states, including available, unavailable, selected and error scenarios.</p>
    <p>I translated these into Gherkin acceptance criteria so design, engineering and QA shared the same expected behaviour.</p>
  </CaseSection>

  <CaseSection eyebrow="Testing what worked" title="Isolated the winning design change" mediaSrc="/images/tv-product-cards/winning-design-change.svg" layout="centered">
    <p>I worked with CRO to test redesigned cards alone (V1) against redesigned cards with filter chips (V2). I kept the explored carousel out of the experiment to avoid introducing another variable.</p>
  </CaseSection>
</CaseDecisionGrid>

<CaseSection eyebrow="A clearer choice that converted" title="21% uplift in XSUS orders" paddingTop="large" paddingBottom="none">
  <p>The filter-chip variant reached 95% statistical confidence, compared with 90% for the card-only variant. This helped isolate the filters as the additional change behind the stronger result and gave the team a pattern to test on other product cards.</p>
</CaseSection>
